## Beginner Tips for Your First Gridlords Demo Run

Tactical games punish impatience more than anything else. These habits will carry you through the demo's opening missions and translate directly to the full release.

| Tip | Why it matters | How to apply it |
|---|---|---|
| Read the turn order bar | Acting out of sequence wastes your strongest abilities | Plan two turns ahead, not one |
| Fight from elevation or cover | Damage and hit chance usually favor high ground | Move first, attack second |
| Don't split your squad | Isolated units get focused down fast | Keep a two-tile buddy system |
| Bank one defensive cooldown | Overkill on turn one leaves you exposed later | Save a heal or escape for emergencies |
| Treat retreat as a real move | Losing a unit costs more than losing tempo | Pull back, heal, re-engage |

A few more habits worth building early:

- **Play the tutorial even if you're a tactics veteran.** It costs ten minutes and usually reveals the specific rules this game bends.
- **Try every available archetype at least once.** The demo roster is small on purpose — each unit teaches you something about the system.
- **Replay the opening mission with a different squad.** Player experience consistently shows that positioning, not raw stats, decides most early fights.

## Unit Roles and Systems You'll Meet

Even a limited demo usually introduces the full spread of combat roles. Knowing what each one is for prevents the classic beginner mistake of building a squad of pure damage dealers.

| Archetype | Primary job | Where to stand |
|---|---|---|
| Frontline bruiser | Absorb hits, hold chokepoints | Front tile, adjacent to cover |
| Ranged damage | Punish exposed targets | Backline with clear line of sight |
| Support / healer | Sustain the squad across turns | Middle, within range of two allies |
| Controller / debuffer | Deny enemy turns or tiles | Flank, behind cover |
| Skirmisher | Finish wounded units | Anywhere with an open path |

### Reading the Grid

Tile types do most of the heavy lifting in games like this. Cover reduces incoming damage, elevation improves your odds, and hazards can be used against enemies as often as they're used against you. Before you move, scan the whole battlefield rather than just your destination — the best tile is usually the one that forces the enemy to come to you.

### Managing the Turn Economy

Action points, cooldowns, and initiative are the real currency of a tactics game. Community reports from demo players often note that the biggest skill jump comes from learning to spend actions efficiently rather than from unlocking new abilities. If an ability can wait a turn without costing you a unit, wait.
