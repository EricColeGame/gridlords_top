Infantry is the spine of nearly every army you will field in Gridlords, and a well-planned **Gridlords infantry build** is the difference between a line that holds and a line that collapses in two turns. Get the frontline right and your ranged units, cavalry, and support pieces get the time they need to do their jobs. Get it wrong and no amount of clever flanking will rescue you.

That is why this guide treats a **Gridlords infantry build** as a system rather than a shopping list. You will see how stats, roles, formation, and upgrade order fit together, plus the habits that quietly lose games for otherwise solid players.

## Why the Infantry Build Decides Most Matches

In grid-based tactics games, the side that controls the most squares usually wins the attrition war. Infantry are the only units that can reliably take a square and keep it. Cavalry hits harder on the charge, and ranged units deal damage from safety, but neither holds ground once the fighting gets close.

A build, in this context, is not just "which unit do I recruit." It is four decisions that compound on each other. Nail all four and your line becomes a wall opponents must spend several turns solving while your damage dealers work. Miss one and the whole structure wobbles.

| Pillar | What it covers | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Stat allocation | Armor, health, melee power, morale | Determines whether a unit survives two hits or five |
| Role mix | Shields, spears, shock, skirmishers | Prevents a single hard counter from gutting your line |
| Formation | Spacing, layering, zone control | Turns five average units into one strong position |
| Upgrade order | Which units get gear and veterancy first | Concentrates power instead of spreading it thin |

Community reports from tactics players tend to agree on one point: infantry that survive are worth more than infantry that hit hard. Damage is replaceable. A dead anvil is not.

## Stat Priorities: What to Actually Invest In

Most new players pour everything into attack and then wonder why their front rank evaporates. In practice, infantry stats are defensive first and offensive second, because a unit that lives one extra round keeps blocking a lane, keeps holding a zone of control, and keeps protecting the units behind it.

| Stat | Priority | What it does for you | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Armor / damage reduction | S-tier | Reduces every incoming hit | Best on your two frontmost units |
| Health / wounds | S-tier | Raises the number of hits needed to remove a unit | Pairs with armor; do not stack one alone |
| Morale / discipline | A-tier | Prevents routs and forced repositioning | Commonly ignored, commonly the reason lines break |
| Melee attack | B-tier | Improves damage and contested rolls | Enough to threaten is enough |
| Movement | B-tier | Repositioning and flank coverage | One or two mobile infantry is plenty |
| Initiative / order | C-tier | Act earlier in the round | Situational; matters most in small fights |

If you only remember one row, remember morale. A unit that runs does not just stop contributing — it opens a gap, and gaps are how good players lose battles they were winning.

## Infantry Roles: Building a Line That Cannot Be Countered

A mono-role line is the fastest way to lose to a prepared opponent. If every unit is a heavy shield, a single armor-piercing or ranged-heavy army picks you apart. If every unit is a damage dealer, a cavalry charge ends the game on turn two.

| Role | Primary job | Common share of a line | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shield / anvil infantry | Hold the center and absorb charges | 40–50% | Durable, forgiving, cheap to maintain | Low damage output |
| Spear / polearm infantry | Punish cavalry, guard flanks | 20–30% | Strong against chargers | Vulnerable to ranged fire |
| Shock / heavy infantry | Break stalemates, push objectives | 15–25% | High burst damage | Expensive and slow |
| Light / skirmish infantry | Screen, harass, grab neutral ground | 10–20% | Cheap, fast, flexible | Folds in a straight melee |
| Support infantry | Banners, buffs, healing | 0–1 unit | Multiplies the whole line | Fragile and a priority target |

Those percentages are a starting point, not gospel. Player experience suggests the ratio shifts toward spears when your opponent leans on cavalry and toward shock infantry when you need to crack a defensive opponent.

## Formation: Where the Build Actually Happens

You can own the best units in the game and still lose because of spacing. Formation is the part of a Gridlords infantry build that most players skip, and it is the part that produces the biggest immediate improvement.

### Keep the line contiguous

Leave no gap wider than a single square in your front rank. Gaps let enemy units slip behind your anvil and reach your ranged core, which usually ends the fight faster than any stat difference.

### Layer a second rank

A second rank of spears directly behind your shields gives you a counter-charge threat. Anyone who commits into your front line has to worry about what steps up next turn.

### Anchor on terrain

Rocks, forests, choke points, and elevation all reduce the number of squares you must defend. A four-unit line anchored on two terrain pieces is functionally a six-unit line.

### Refuse the flanks

Angle your outermost units slightly back so they cannot be surrounded on the first contact. Trading a little offensive reach for a protected flank is almost always correct for infantry.

## Upgrade and Veterancy Order

Spreading upgrades evenly across ten units produces ten mediocre units. Concentrating them produces two or three units that win fights alone. Here is the order that tends to perform best.

| Priority | Investment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Armor upgrades on your most-hit units | Damage reduction pays off every single round |
| 2 | Morale upgrades on the center | A rout cascades faster than a death |
| 3 | Weapon upgrades on shock infantry | They exist to end fights, not survive them |
| 4 | Mobility gear on light infantry | Lets you contest objectives and screen |
| 5 | Support upgrades | Last, unless your whole plan leans on buffs |

Veterancy follows the same logic. Feed kills and safe experience to the units you intend to keep alive, and let expendable light infantry screen them. An elite anvil is a genuine win condition in most Gridlords matches.

## A Simple Turn-by-Turn Plan

| Phase | Focus | Target outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Deployment | Anchored line, refused flanks | No gaps wider than one square |
| Turns 1–2 | Advance as a block, keep formation | Contest central objectives |
| Turns 3–4 | Absorb the charge, counter with spears | Trade cheap units for expensive ones |
| Turns 5+ | Push with shock infantry | Break the line, pursue with cavalry |

## Common Mistakes That Break a Good Build

- **Over-investing in attack.** Damage does not hold squares; durability does.
- **Spreading upgrades thin.** Three strong units beat ten average ones.
- **Ignoring morale.** The most common cause of a sudden collapse.
- **Chasing kills with the anvil.** Your shields should hold position, not sprint forward.
- **Bringing too much infantry.** Without ranged or cavalry support, you cannot convert a won fight into a won battle.
- **Forgetting the objective.** Holding ground matters more than trading casualties evenly.

## FAQ

**How many infantry should a Gridlords infantry build include?**

A common starting split is roughly half your force in durable anvil infantry, a quarter in spears, and the rest split between shock and light units, plus at most one support piece. Adjust toward spears against cavalry-heavy opponents.

**Is the build viable without cavalry or ranged support?**

It can hold, but it usually cannot win. Infantry wins the attrition phase; cavalry and ranged units convert that advantage into map control and kills. Treat infantry as the foundation, not the entire house.

**Which stat should I upgrade first?**

Armor, then health, then morale on your center units. Those three determine how many rounds your line survives, and survival is what buys your damage dealers time.

**Why does my line break even with good armor?**

Almost always morale or spacing. Check for gaps wider than one square and for units that are isolated from the rest of the line. A unit fighting alone loses morale faster than one fighting shoulder to shoulder.

Build the wall first, then the sword. A disciplined **Gridlords infantry build** will not win every match on its own, but it will keep you in every match long enough to find the one mistake that decides it.
